# Baseline plan (test F and external benchmark tools)

M0 plan, 2026-10-15. How the baselines are run and measured. External tools are run as separate programs only: never linked, never copied, never vendored, and their source code is never opened by builder agents (brief, originality 5). They are installed outside the repository, in a scratch folder such as `..\bench-tools\`, with their exact versions recorded in the benchmark report (docs/BENCHMARKS.md, Evani.Model section, written in M5).

## 1. Baseline F: the same agent without Evani.Model

**Goal (brief, test F and D).** Compare quality, calls, time and tokens for 10 benchmark intents made by the same agent model writing raw SDF or Blender Python, against Evani.Model (target: at least 5x fewer tokens and calls at equal or better quality).

**The 10 intents** (spread over the three groups and the hard cases): `crate`, `barrel`, `lamp_post`, `door`, `chest`, `sword`, `kart_01_pip`, `kart_08_rumble`, `tower`, `mushroom`.

**Two arms, same agent, same effort.**
- **F-Blender:** the agent writes Blender Python, run in headless Blender (`blender --background --python`, or the Blender MCP server's command-line execution), and may ask for renders. Output: a glTF export of the final object.
- **F-SDF:** the agent writes one signed distance function in WGSL (`fn sdf(p: vec3f) -> f32`), evaluated and meshed by a neutral harness at a fixed resolution (plain marching cubes of our own, no search, templates, style, solver, relief or LODs). It may ask for a render of the current mesh.

**Same inputs for every run.** The intent text (as an English description plus the `.evmodel` itself), the reference masks for its views, the must tests in plain words, the quality bar in plain words (below), and the same generous caps: at most 40 tool calls or 90 minutes per intent, whichever comes first; same model and effort as the Evani.Model run (planned: Opus at high effort). Neither baseline arm gets Evani.Model's checks or IoU numbers during the run: they get pictures (which may show the reference mask as an outline behind the model, as a creator would place a reference image) and bounding boxes. The agent works until it says it is done or a cap is reached.

**Measured to a fixed bar, not at a fixed cap (fixes the review's finding M5b).** The bar is the same for every arm and for Evani.Model: every must test passes, the gate IoU (MODEL_API.md 1.7) is 0.9 or better in every reference view, and the mesh is watertight, manifold and free of self-intersections. The harness scores the agent's current output silently after every call, so a run is credited at the *first* call after which its output meets the bar, even if the agent carries on; calls, tokens and time are counted up to that call. A run that never meets the bar is reported as "not reached", with its numbers at the cap as lower bounds. Why the cap changed: with the old cap of 12 calls and Evani.Model's median of 2, no comparison could show more than a 6x gap, and a run stopped below the bar would compare unequal quality; with 40 calls the measurable gap is 20x, and comparing at the bar makes "at equal or better quality" true by construction. The share of runs that never reach the bar is reported per arm and per intent, and the ratio is stated as "at least" when any baseline run is not reached.

**Scoring (identical for all).** The output mesh goes through our scoring: silhouette IoU per view in the item's fixed frame; Chamfer distance and F1 against the reference outer surface (where one exists); must tests evaluated on the mesh (converted to a field by our mesh-to-field step); watertight, manifold and self-intersection checks (our own meshcheck, note 13, cross-checked with geocheck's); triangle count. Efficiency: tool calls, input and output tokens (from the session transcripts' usage records), wall time, GPU time where measurable.

**Repeats.** 3 runs per intent per arm (60 runs); report the median and spread. Evani.Model runs the same 10 intents 3 times too (its model is deterministic at a given level, so the spread is in agent tokens only).

**Where and how heavy.** On the dev PC (Standard tier), one run at a time, Blender at below-normal priority with its audio off. Estimated 25 to 40 hours of agent time in total with the larger cap (about 90 hours in the worst case, if every run used its full 90 minutes); tokens are the main cost (decision D9 covers the budget).

**Licence.** Blender is GPL-3.0-or-later; it is run as a separate program; generated scripts are our outputs and are kept only in the run logs (`.evani/bench/`, gitignored), never in the engine.

## 2. External tools as benchmark baselines

| Tool | Licence | Compared with | How it is run | Measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| meshoptimizer (gltfpack command line) | MIT | our per-tier LODs (note 14) | Simplify our Ultra mesh to each tier's triangle count, and separately search the ratio that matches our silhouette error (bisection, 8 steps) | Triangles at equal silhouette error (target: ours at least 30% fewer), silhouette error at equal triangles, Chamfer distance, time |
| xatlas (its command-line example or the MIT-licensed Python bindings) | MIT | our charts and packing (note 16) | Unwrap our meshes with the UVs removed, default settings and a tuned setting | Chart count, total and visible seam length (visible from the class's typical views), area and angle distortion, packing efficiency, time |
| CoACD (its command line or Python package) | MIT | our collision proxies (note 15) | Decompose our Ultra mesh (exported as OBJ by our tool) at the default concavity threshold and at a threshold tuned to a similar hull count | Hull count, total vertices, volume error against the model (sampled symmetric difference), probe-point contact error, time |
| TRELLIS.2 | MIT (code and model); depends on nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec under the NVIDIA Source Code License (non-commercial) | our organic items (tree, rock, mushroom, cloud, bush, cactus, gumdrop, chibi body) | Image-to-3D on a cloud Linux GPU with at least 24 GB (its README: Linux, CUDA, 24 GB minimum), input: a shaded render of our reference assembly (our own image) | IoU per view, Chamfer and F1 against the reference, triangle count, watertight and manifold, time, GPU memory; evaluation only, its outputs are never published or used in games |
| Khronos glTF-Validator | Apache-2.0 | every compiled file | Command line on every `.glb` | Errors and warnings (target zero) |
| Blender (glTF exporter's tangents) | GPL-3.0-or-later | our MikkTSpace-convention tangents (note 16) | Import our mesh, export with tangents, compare numerically | Maximum and mean tangent angle difference, handedness agreement |

**Setup notes.** This PC has no Python today; the Python-based tools (CoACD, xatlas bindings) need a Python install in the scratch folder (no effect on the repository). gltfpack and glTF-Validator are single executables. TRELLIS.2 cannot run locally (8 GB GPU, Windows), so it needs a cloud machine (decision D7). All tools run one at a time at below-normal priority on the shared PC.

**Fairness rules.** Default settings first, then one tuned setting chosen to match the comparison point (equal triangles, equal hull count); both reported. The tool's version and command line are recorded with every number.

## 3. Ablations (test G), for completeness
Rerun the full benchmark with each turned off in turn: search (one candidate at range centres), multi-fidelity scoring (all candidates at the top rung in the same time), learned priors, word calibration (even default ranges), retrieval (no `model.describe` before writing intents), edit verification, stronger critic (builder judges itself). Report quality (must pass rate, IoU, edit success) and efficiency (calls, tokens, time) for each. Run in M5 on the dev PC.

## 4. Test D, defined so it can be measured
Added in the M0 follow-up (review finding M5). Every number below is computed by the benchmark runner from logged data, never estimated by hand.
- **A finished model** meets the bar of section 1 (every must passes, gate IoU 0.9 or better per reference view, mesh valid) and is compiled for every tier with the glTF validator and our meshcheck clean.
- **Calls** are MCP tool calls (Evani.Model) or tool calls (baselines) from the first call to the one that produced the finished model, sheets and describes included. **Tokens** are the agent's input and output tokens over the same turns, from the session transcript's usage records; input is reported as uncached and cache-read separately, and the 5x target is checked on both. **Wall time** runs from the first request to the last reply. **GPU time** is the sum of GPU timestamp queries over the calls where the adapter supports them, otherwise reported as not available.
- **"At least 5x fewer tokens and calls at equal or better quality"**: per F intent, the median of the baseline runs divided by the median of the Evani.Model runs, both measured to the same bar; passed when the ratio of the pooled medians is 5 or more for both calls and tokens; every intent's own ratio is listed next to it (a proposed reading of the brief, open to Chris). Quality is equal by construction at the bar; the final IoU and Chamfer distance after each run are also reported.
- **Candidates needed, falling as priors grow.** For each intent, *candidates to target* is the smallest ladder level (32, 64, ... 1024) whose result meets the bar; the levels share their prefix of candidates, so this costs little more than the largest level tried. Priors in a benchmark run come only from the run itself, through the benchmark acceptance rule (note 09, decision D18). The test is run in two fixed orders (the manifest's order and its reverse), each with priors on and off and with the intents' `like` lines identical in both arms, so the difference is the priors alone (seven of the eight karts say `like: bench/kart_01_pip`, which otherwise would dominate). Passed when, in both orders, the priors-on arm needs fewer candidates than the priors-off arm on the median of the second half of the run.
- **LODs with at least 30% fewer triangles than a generic simplifier at equal silhouette error.** Silhouette error is 1 minus the gate IoU of the tier mesh against the Ultra field from the class's typical views at the tier's resolution and view distance. gltfpack's ratio is found by bisection (8 steps) to match our error on each tier; passed when, on every tier, the median of our triangle count over gltfpack's across the benchmark models is 70% or less; every model's ratio is listed (a proposed reading of the brief, open to Chris).
- **Bytes per tier**: raw `.glb` bytes per tier, and after evani-convert once it exists (Stage 3).
