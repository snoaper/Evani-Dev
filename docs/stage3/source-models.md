# Stage 3 source models (Meshy)

Date: 2026-10-16. Seven AI-made models for the Stage 3 converter and the S3-16 test character. Docs only: the model files are large and are never committed.

**Licence:** made by Chris on a paid Meshy plan; he owns them, commercial use is allowed, no credit line needed. Do not publish or upload them anywhere without Chris asking.

**Where they live:** `art-source/meshy/<id>/` in the main checkout (gitignored, `/art-source/`). Each folder holds the untouched Meshy files, `<id>.master.glb` (untouched master export) and `<id>.report.json` (the full measurements). `art-source/meshy/README.md` (also gitignored) lists the same. Originals are in Chris's Downloads folder. Previews: `.vibedeck/screenshots/meshy-models/<id>.png` (front, side, back, three-quarter; gitignored).

**How they were made:** Blender 5.2.2 LTS headless (pin in `docs/stage3/spikes.md`), script `tools/blender/meshy_prepare.py`: import (FBX for the Marshal and the Chibi, OBJ+MTL for the rest; the Marshal's base colour, metallic, roughness and normal maps wired by hand), measure, export glTF binary (+Y up, extras on, PBR materials and textures embedded, nothing simplified), render the sheet. Validated with `gltf-validator` 2.0.0-dev.3.10 (`tools/blender/validate_glb.mjs`).

## Roles and what each still needs

- **`frontier-marshal`: the Stage 3 AI-made test character** (STAGE3_PLAN decision 10, S3-16): becomes the kart racer's driver, rigged in Blender with idle, steering lean, look back and celebrate clips. Needs before S3-16: a rig (it has none; the T-pose suits it), possibly a pass to separate arms and hands for the steering pose. At 19.5k triangles it is already near a driver budget for the top tier; the converter's lower tiers need the decimation. The four PBR maps are 2048 to 4096; the metallic and roughness maps will be packed into one and shrunk by the converter.
- **`mustached-strongman`**: extra converter test model, low-poly like the Marshal (20.6k triangles), T-pose, so it could also take a rig test. Needs: nothing for conversion tests.
- **`statue-of-liberty`**: extra converter test model, static, 9.9k triangles, hard-edged detail (pedestal) good for checking silhouette and texture quality. Needs: nothing.
- **`chibi-figure`**, **`pumpkin-fiend`**, **`realistic-cowgirl`**: dense (430k, 570k, 760k triangles) sculpt-style meshes: good stress tests for the converter's decimation and "can nobody tell the difference" check. Rigging them would need retopology first. The Cowgirl is a T-pose, the best of these for a later rig.
- **`anxious-adventurer`: 3.1 million triangles, 8192 px texture.** The master .glb is 187 MB, below the 200 MB limit, so it is practical to keep but not to load in a game or in a browser test as is. Import into Blender took 6 s. It is a converter stress test only (decimation by a factor of about 100 to 1,000 for any real tier); no rig plan.

None of the dense models is fit to ship as is; every one goes through the converter (S3-5) and the gate checks.

## Frontier Marshal: rig and clips (S3-16, decision 10)

Date: 2026-10-16. Rigged master `art-source/meshy/frontier-marshal/frontier-marshal.rigged.master.glb` (19.5 MB, gitignored) and its Blender file `frontier-marshal.rig.blend`; the untouched master is kept as it was. Scripts: `tools/blender/marshal_rig.py` (rig, weights, anchors, clips, export; deterministic) and `tools/blender/marshal_preview.py` (preview sheets in `.vibedeck/screenshots/marshal-rig2/`, gitignored; the first rig's are in `marshal-rig/`), both run headless with the pinned Blender (`-b --factory-startup -t 2`). Validator: 0 errors; warnings are the generated tangents and `NODE_SKINNED_MESH_NON_ROOT` (the mesh sits under the armature node; harmless), infos `NODE_EMPTY` (the anchors).

**Scale and frame.** Scaled by **1.15** from the Meshy master (1.90 m), so he stands 2.185 m. Reason, from `draw_kart` (kart-local metres): seat top 0.64 at z -0.30, wheel centre (0, 0.98, 0.34), radius 0.23, pitched 1.0 rad; at 1.15 the arms reach the 9:30/2:30 grips with a forward racing lean, and the seated hat top is about 1.85 m, under the placeholder helmet top (2.02) and the player marker (2.3). Units are metres; glTF frame +X his left, +Y up, +Z forward (the kart's frame). Origin: the ground under him when standing; in the seated clips the ground under the seat contact.

**Skeleton: 46 joints** (soft cap 64, S3-9), all deform except `root`, which is exported as the skin root: `root`, `hips`, `spine`, `spine1`, `spine2`, `neck`, `head`, `hat`, `coat_back` and `coat_tail` (a two-bone flap for the back of the coat skirt, under `hips`); per side (`_l` = his left, +X; `_r`): `shoulder`, `upper_arm`, `forearm`, `hand`, `fingers1`, `fingers2`, `fingers3` (all four fingers as one curl), `thumb1`, `thumb2`, `thigh`, `shin`, `foot`, `toe`, and five short sleeve-fringe bones `fringe1`-`fringe5` under the arm (1-2 on `upper_arm`, 3-5 on `forearm`, each 12 cm long pointing down from the strand roots). Bone Y runs along the bone; arms and hands have Z up in the T-pose (back of the hand), legs Z forward.

**Weights.** Bone heat on a welded copy (the glTF import splits vertices on seams), copied back vertex by vertex, then: hand and fingers only past the cuff and the cuff only on the arm (the wrist twist hides inside the cuff); every sleeve fringe vertex takes a smooth profile of the sleeve underside's weights along the arm (Gaussian in x, sigma 15 mm), so each strand is rigid and the next differs only a little (no tearing between strands when the arm goes up); below a 5 cm root band each strand then hands over to the fringe bones (linear between the two nearest along the arm), applied after the seam smoothing so no stray forearm weight reaches the strand tips; every clip turns each fringe bone 85 percent of the way from where the arm carries it toward straight down, so with the arm raised the strands hang down along the sleeve instead of fanning out sideways (the engine skins without shape keys, hence bones); the chest and back yoke fringes beside the armpit hang from the chest (`spine2`, `spine1`, `shoulder`, no arm bones); head, hair and beard rigid on `head` above the jaw line, blended into the bone-heat weights over 5 cm so the nape and beard don't tear from the collar, the hat on `hat` blending over the brim; the back panel of the coat skirt on `hips` at the waist and the `coat_back` / `coat_tail` flap below (bone heat gave it to the thighs, which swung it down through the seat); a few smoothing passes over the seams those rules leave (sleeve / fringe / yoke, nape, flap edges); at most 4 influences, slivers under 0.01 dropped. In the seated clips the flap tucks under him and the tail lies back on the cushion, so the coat line runs smoothly onto the seat.

**Anchors** (empty nodes; at bind their axes match the model frame): `anchor:hand_l`, `anchor:hand_r` (children of the hand bones, the grip point under the palm centre; two-bone IK targets for the wheel), `anchor:head_look` (child of `head`, between the eyes; aim IK, looks along +Z), `anchor:seat` (child of `root`, 0.64 m up; the hip contact in the seated clips). For the current kart the kart's seat anchor is kart (0, 0.64, -0.30), so the driver's origin lands under the seat.

**Clips** (standard glTF animations, 30 fps, every frame sampled, root never moves; loops have the last frame equal to the first; `extras.evani.clips` on the scene lists them):

| clip | frames | seconds | loop | what |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `idle_seated` | 73 | 2.4 | yes | seated, hands on the wheel, breathing, small head and hat movement |
| `lean_left` | 73 | 2.4 | yes | blend-space end: body leans left, wheel turned 0.9 rad left (the kit's full visual turn) |
| `lean_center` | 73 | 2.4 | yes | blend-space middle: straight driving, breathing |
| `lean_right` | 73 | 2.4 | yes | blend-space end, mirrored |
| `look_back` | 40 | 1.3 | no | over the right shoulder: hips 14, chest 44 and head 90 degrees round, rolled onto the left hip; turns in 0.3 s, holds 0.7 s, turns back in 0.3 s; hands stay on the rim, sliding round it to where the turned chest reaches |
| `stand_idle` | 73 | 2.4 | yes | podium stand, arms down, breathing and a small sway |
| `celebrate` | 76 | 2.5 | no | starts on `stand_idle` frame 0: right fist punched straight up (86 degrees at the top of each pump), left fist on the hip, chest thrown back and hips twisting with each of three pumps, a deep knee dip between them; both fists up in a V for the last pump, held, then back to standing |

The three lean clips share one length and breathing phase, so the 1D blend space can phase-sync them. Seated: hips over `anchor:seat`, knees up and apart either side of the steering column, feet forward at the pedals (ankles at kart z 0.50, partly under the nose fairing as in a real kart).

**Known limits** (measured by the rig check, `tools/blender/rig_check/frontier-marshal.json`; the polish review of 2026-10-16 compared against the first rig). Hands: the anchor-to-rim gap is 0 mm in every seated clip and at most 2.2 mm (mean 0.8) for the right hand in `look_back` (was 37 mm at full lean and 106 mm in `look_back`). Seat: 0 vertices sink into the cushion or seat back in any seated clip (was 1,076, 80 mm deep), and none reach below the seat top (an "under the seat" box, floor to 0.60 m); the coat's fringed hem lies flat on the cushion behind his hips. The hand-gap limit is 5 mm, so a hand drifting 10 mm off the rim fails. Sleeve fringes hang: every strand edge below the root band stays within 34 degrees of straight down in the seated, `look_back` and standing clips and 37 in `celebrate` (rig check `hang`), with a worst per-frame mean of 16 degrees in the seated, `look_back` and standing clips and 19 in `celebrate` with an arm straight up; before the fringe bones, measured the same way, the mean was 118 degrees in `celebrate` (the strands fanned up and out like a stiff comb) and 45-96 degrees in the other clips. Their worst edge stretch per clip, in the root band: `stand_idle` 1.7x, seated clips 1.7-3.0x, `look_back` 1.8-4.0x, `celebrate` 3.5-4.0x (first rig 9.6-22x, before the fringe bones 5.4x); below it at most 1.9x (before the fringe bones 6.6x). The 2-5 mm root-band edges, where a strand hands over from the sleeve to its fringe bone, stretch where the sleeve bends; a wider weight profile (40 mm instead of 15) and a longer root band (7 cm: 4.6x) were tried and were worse. The inside of that fold shortens (down to 0.24x standing, 0.03x in `celebrate`). Below the root band, the thin webs that join neighbouring strands close up to 0.02x in `celebrate`: with the arm straight up, a strand rooted further out hangs from higher up, so a web running from one strand's root to the next strand's lower part folds like a pleated curtain while each strand keeps its length (its strand edges stay at 0.50-1.40x in `celebrate` and 0.69-1.24x in the other clips, checked by `hang`); in the other clips the strands keep at least 0.36x. The back of each armpit pinches 1-5 mm edges up to 28.6x when the arms come down from the T-pose source; it sits under the arm and the first rig had the same. The config's limits are those numbers plus about 15 percent, each with its reason in `about`; they are a regression guard, not the goal (1.3x), and the sleeve-fringe root stretch (worst in `celebrate` and `look_back`) stays an open defect, not an accepted one. A corrective shape or a re-topologised armpit would close the rest once the engine skins meshes.

## Rig check (any rigged character)

`tools/blender/rig_check.py` measures a rigged model's clips against limits in a small JSON config, so a deformation error found on one character becomes a check every character runs (Chris's "errors become tools" rule; it came out of the Marshal polish review). It reads a `.glb` or `.blend`, plays every clip (or the ones each entry names) frame by frame and reports, per clip:

- **Anchor gaps** (`anchor_gaps`): the distance from a named anchor (for example `anchor:hand_l`) to a target, max and mean in mm over the clip, with the worst frame. A target is another anchor, a fixed point or a ring (centre, normal, radius: a steering-wheel rim, a handlebar loop).
- **Collider sink** (`colliders`): vertices that sink deeper than `ignore_mm` inside a box (centre, size, optional rotation; a seat, a saddle, a floor), with the count, the deepest sink and which bones those vertices follow, with their rest-pose centre so the report says where.
- **Stretch** (`stretch`): vertices whose connected edges grow or shrink past `max_ratio` / `min_ratio` of their rest length (changes under `min_change_mm` ignored), with the worst five edges and the frames of the worst stretch and the worst shrink. A rest-pose `region` box or `only_groups` narrows it to, say, the sleeve fringes.
- **Hang** (`hang`): for strands that should hang (fringes, tassels, hair ends), the angle from straight down of every strand edge, so no strand has to be found (fused strands join into a few pieces, and pairing tips with roots by distance picks the wrong strand). A strand edge lies in the selection below its top `root_mm` (default 15; the surface the strands hang from and the band where they bend with it), is at least `edge_mm` long (default 3) and within `rest_deg` (default 20) of straight down at rest; webs between strands run across and are not measured. Each frame takes each strand edge from its upper to its lower end as at rest. It reports the number of strand edges, the worst edge angle and the worst per-frame mean per clip, and the smallest and largest strand-edge length ratio, and fails past `max_deg` or outside `min_len_ratio` / `max_len_ratio` (counting edges, `max_count`), or past `max_mean_deg`. The length ratios catch a strand that collapses or stretches even where the webs between strands may fold. Stiff strands that fan out sideways when an arm goes up show here even when no edge stretches.

Run: `blender -b --factory-startup -t 2 --python tools/blender/rig_check.py -- <model> <config.json> [--out report.json] [--report-only] [--clips a,b]`. It prints one `RIG_CHECK {json}` line per clip and check and `RIG_CHECK_RESULT pass|fail`; it exits 1 on a fail unless `--report-only`. All positions in the config are in the model's glTF frame (+Y up, metres); the format is documented at the top of the script. A new character copies `tools/blender/rig_check/frontier-marshal.json`, changes the anchors, targets and boxes to its own props, and sets the limits; a limit above the goal needs a one-line reason in the config's `about`.
