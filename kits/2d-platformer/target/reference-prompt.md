# Reference image prompt

```
In-game screenshot from an original 2D platformer video game, side-on 2D camera following the hero, the ground the hero stands on in the lower third of the screen and open sky above.
A small fox-eared hero in a teal scarf leaps over a pool on a grassy hillside, a short arc of gold coins following the jump, a shelled beetle patrolling the far bank, a stone pillar with a mossy cap ahead and a hilltop castle on the horizon. Earth below the grass darkens with depth and shows roots, pebbles and a buried bone. A small "100" floats up above a stomped enemy, clear of the hero. HUD across the top: lives, coin count, score, timer.
Art style: 16-bit pixel. Richer palettes, layered parallax, detailed sprite animation, every pixel the same size.
Crisp, polished 2D at native resolution: clean edges, readable silhouettes, rich colour and lighting where the style allows.
16:9 widescreen game capture, 1920×1080 (480×270 art pixels at 4×). Everything is original: characters, creatures, logos and UI. No existing game franchises, characters, brands or real people, and no watermark. Any on-screen text is short and legible.
```

## What a capture is compared on

1. **Framing**: the hero inside the middle of the frame; when standing, the ground line at about 72 % of the height, so terrain below the feet covers at most about 35 % of the screen. Sky and the way ahead fill the rest.
2. **Pixel integrity**: one pixel size everywhere (tiles, sprites, parallax, floating text, HUD), at whole-pixel positions; no blurred or half pixels.
3. **Terrain**: soil darkens with depth; roots, rocks and buried details break the pattern; the stone pillar reads as built (cap, shaft, plinth).
4. **Readability**: coins clear of decor and terrain, on the path of a jump; hazards (spikes, water) visible, never behind decor; floating text never on the hero or on other text.
5. **Palette**: only the colours of [look-bible.md](look-bible.md); outlines in `ink`; top-left light.
